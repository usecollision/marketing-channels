## When to Use

Invoke when:
- Outreach emails to creators read like mass spam and response rates are near zero
- Compensation conversations happen without structure — you anchor on the creator's first number
- Proven creators do one campaign and disappear instead of becoming long-term partners
- You need to negotiate usage rights, whitelisting, or Spark Ads arrangements
- Creator relationships need a pipeline: research, sequence, negotiation, contract, renewal

## Workflow

### Step 1: Build and Prioritize the Creator List
- [ ] Research each creator before outreach: last 5 posts, audience, brand deals they ran, what they clearly care about
- [ ] Bucket by tier and goal (see influencer-marketing for scoring) — outreach tone and offer differ per tier
- [ ] Find the personal angle: reference a specific post, opinion, or audience insight in the first touch
- [ ] Prioritize 3-5x more creators than you need — response rates on cold creator outreach are low (heuristic — track yours and update the plan)
- [ ] Track everything in a CRM with stages: researched → contacted → negotiating → contracted → live → renewal

**Gate:** Prioritized list with per-creator research notes in the CRM.

### Step 2: Run the Outreach Sequence
- [ ] Touch 1 — the hook: who you are, why them specifically (one concrete reference to their content), the audience win for them, and a soft ask (20 minutes or a simple yes/no). Short email, no attachments
- [ ] Touch 2 (3-4 days later) — value first: send something useful (product access, data, a resource for their audience) without re-pitching hard
- [ ] Touch 3 (a week later) — the easy yes: a small paid test or a low-commitment first collaboration, then a polite stop rule — after 3 touches with no reply, park them for 3 months (heuristic)
- [ ] Use the channel creators live on — DMs work where email doesn't, but email first for anything involving contracts
- [ ] Never mass-batch: a researched personal line per creator is the entire game (see cold-email-sequence for structure discipline)

**Gate:** Sequence defined with 3 touches and a stop rule; outreach running with per-creator personalization.

### Step 3: Negotiate Compensation
- [ ] Model options: flat fee (fixed cost, simplest), affiliate or rev share (performance-aligned, variable), product access plus fee (common for nano/micro), hybrid base plus performance bonus
- [ ] Anchor with a written range tied to deliverables, not to the creator's follower count alone — audience fit and past results move the price
- [ ] Negotiate rights in the same conversation as price: usage duration, channels, paid amplification — price without rights is an incomplete deal
- [ ] Leave budget headroom: creators often counter; know your ceiling before the call
- [ ] For long-term interest, show the path: what a successful test unlocks (retainer, bigger campaign, affiliate upside)

**Gate:** Compensation model and ceiling agreed per creator; rights negotiated alongside price.

### Step 4: Contract and Deliverables
- [ ] Contract essentials: deliverables and deadlines, compensation and payment terms, usage rights and duration, exclusivity windows, disclosure obligations, termination
- [ ] Keep contracts simple — creators abandon deals buried in legal density (assumption)
- [ ] Align on approval flow: number of revision rounds and who approves — avoid death by committee
- [ ] Schedule payment reliably — the fastest way to lose a creator relationship is paying late
- [ ] File every contract; the library is your precedent base for the next negotiation

**Gate:** Contracts signed with deliverables, rights, and payment terms explicit.

### Step 5: Grow Long-Term Partnerships
- [ ] After the campaign: share results with the creator, credit their impact, and pay on time — basic, and rare enough to differentiate
- [ ] Identify proven performers and move them up: repeat campaign → quarterly retainer → ambassador or affiliate deal (see affiliate-program, ambassador-program)
- [ ] Give top partners first-party insights: audience data, product roadmap previews, early access — information is a benefit that costs little
- [ ] Manage the portfolio with tiers: invest in the top, maintain the middle, let the rest lapse (see partnership-strategy)
- [ ] Review the roster quarterly: who over-delivered, who burned trust, who grew into a bigger tier

**Gate:** Partner tiering applied, retainers offered to proven performers, quarterly roster review scheduled.

### Step 6: Set Up Whitelisting and Spark Ads
- [ ] Whitelisting mechanics: the creator grants the brand permission to run paid ads through their handle — the ad shows as from the creator, not the brand
- [ ] Spark Ads (Meta): brand boosts an existing organic creator post with the creator's authorization — the mechanics and permission splits must be written into the contract
- [ ] Decide when it's worth it: proven organic posts that already performed are the strongest candidates (assumption — validate against your own post data)
- [ ] Agree the economics up front: flat licensing fee, media budget coverage, or performance share
- [ ] Set the guardrails: which posts can be boosted, for how long, with what targeting — creators protect their audience trust, respect it

**Gate:** Whitelisting terms in the contract template, Spark Ads permission process defined, economics and guardrails agreed.

## Evaluation & QA

### Scoring Rubric
| Criteria | Score 1 | Score 3 | Score 5 |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| List quality | Scraped usernames | Researched notes | Prioritized 3-5x list, staged in CRM |
| Outreach | Mass template | Structured sequence | 3 touches, personal references, value-first, stop rule |
| Negotiation | Accepts the ask | Prepared ceiling | Price + rights negotiated together with a growth path |
| Contracts | Verbal agreement | Simple contract | Rights, exclusivity, payment terms explicit and filed |
| Longevity | One-off deals | Repeat campaigns | Tiered portfolio + retainers + quarterly roster review |

### Common Failure Modes
- Mass-blast outreach that ignores everything the creator actually makes
- Negotiating price before scope — every deliverable addition reopens the price
- Forgetting usage rights until the post goes viral and you can't use it
- Late or sloppy payment — creators talk to each other
- Whitelisting with no contract — permission disputes after the campaign is running
- Treating every creator identically instead of tiering the portfolio
- No renewal cadence — proven partners lapse for lack of a next step
